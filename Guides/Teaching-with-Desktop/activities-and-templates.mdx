## First session: predict, compare, explain

**Suggested duration:** 45 minutes, after the student access check. Allow more time if students are also learning the interface.

**Learning objective:** Students can explain how one selected simulator adjustment affects the resulting image, using two images as evidence.

**Format:** Guided practice, individually or in pairs. In pairs, swap the operator and observer roles so both students use the controls and explain a comparison.

**Instructor preparation:** Choose and rehearse one case and one control, such as collimation. Specify the starting setup, what should remain unchanged, and the image features students should examine. Prepare a brief explanation of relevant simulator limitations.

| Time | What happens |
| --- | --- |
| 5 minutes | Introduce the objective, relevant prior learning, and how students will receive feedback. Explain that the task includes prediction, observation, and discussion. |
| 5 minutes | Demonstrate the selected control, the image comparison, and export. |
| 20 minutes | Students make a prediction, change one control, save their images, and record an explanation. Pairs swap roles halfway through. |
| 10 minutes | Discuss selected comparisons, including differences between predictions and observations. |
| 5 minutes | Each student submits a short explanation and one remaining question. |

### Student task

1. Open the instructor's case and reproduce the starting setup.
2. Save an initial image. Record the selected control's value and other settings needed to reproduce the comparison.
3. Predict what will happen when you make the specified adjustment.
4. Change that control only, keeping the other settings and image processing mode unchanged. Save a second image.
5. Compare the images using the criteria discussed in class. Explain what changed and what you would investigate next.

Use **Save image** and locate the exported files as described in the [Desktop quick start](/Guides/Introduction/getting-started-Desktop-x-ray). Submit through the institution's usual assignment process, using the filenames and location given by the instructor.

### Discussion prompts

- What did you predict, and what did you observe?
- Which feature of the images supports your explanation?
- Did anything else change that might affect the comparison?
- What adjustment would you try next, and why?
- Which parts of the result need further explanation from your instructor?

## Activities to add to your course

### Collimation comparison

**Objective:** Explain how two collimation choices compare with the anatomy coverage criteria taught in the course.

**Task:** Starting from the instructor's setup, save an image, adjust the field size, and save a second image. Keep patient position, tube position, technique settings, and image processing mode unchanged. Identify anatomy included or excluded in each image and explain which result better meets the stated criteria.

**Submit:** Two images, the field dimensions used, and a short explanation. The instructor supplies the criteria and discusses whether the comparison isolates the intended change.

### Positioning investigation

**Objective:** Explain the visible effect of one supported positioning adjustment.

**Task:** Use the case and movement selected by the instructor. Predict the effect, change one positioning control, and compare the resulting image with the starting image. Record the adjustment and keep other settings unchanged. Choose the next adjustment you would make and explain your reasoning.

**Submit:** Two images, the adjustment made, and an explanation linked to the course's positioning criteria. Faculty confirm that the movement and intended comparison are supported in that case.

### One technique parameter at a time

**Objective:** Describe the observed image response when one technique parameter changes in a fixed simulator setup.

**Task:** Use the case, image mode, starting setup, and parameter values provided by the instructor. Change either mAs or kVp while keeping the other parameter, positioning, collimation, and processing settings unchanged. Save and label the comparison images, then relate the observations to the concept taught in class.

**Submit:** The images, a record of the settings, and a short explanation. Faculty rehearse the comparison in the selected image mode and discuss its limits. The exercise uses instructor-selected simulation settings, not a patient exposure protocol.

## Give feedback on the reasoning

Use these prompts for formative feedback. Faculty can adapt them to the course's existing assessment approach.

| Review point | Evidence to look for |
| --- | --- |
| Clear comparison | The student identifies the case, changed control, and settings held constant. |
| Specific observation | The student points to visible image features rather than only saying an image is better or worse. |
| Explanation | The student connects the observation to the concept and criteria taught in class. |
| Next step | The student proposes an adjustment or question and explains why it follows from the result. |

After the first two activities, return to [Review before expanding](/Guides/Teaching-with-Desktop/plan-your-course-rollout#5-review-before-expanding) to update the course plan.
