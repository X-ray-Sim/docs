Use this activity after teaching wrist anatomy and the coverage required for a PA wrist image. It gives students a focused question to investigate: **which anatomy is missing, and what would you change?**

## Wrist collimation: what is missing?

**Learning objective:** Students identify anatomy excluded by collimation and explain how they would adjust the field to include it.

**Case:** Healthy wrist, PA view. The [wrist reference](/Simulation-Catalog/upper-extremity/wrist) describes the available anatomy and projections.

**Format:** Guided practice in pairs, with one computer per pair. You can also demonstrate the activity to the class using a projector.

**Suggested time:** 45 minutes. This is a planning suggestion; adjust it after trying the activity, especially if students are new to the controls.

### Before class

Try the activity and save your own comparison images. Start with the wrist positioned and centred using the criteria you teach. Make sure the initial image includes the required anatomy.

Find **Technique**, then the **Collimator cutoff** controls. These change the field size in two directions. For this activity, students use one of those controls and leave positioning, kVp, mAs, and image processing settings unchanged.

If you need help finding the controls or saving an image, use the [Desktop quick start](/Guides/Introduction/getting-started-Desktop-x-ray).

### A suggested class plan

| Time | What to do |
| --- | --- |
| 10 minutes | Introduce the task. Show the starting image, review the required anatomy, and demonstrate the collimation control and **Save image**. |
| 20 minutes | Students complete the comparison in pairs. Ask them to swap roles so both use the controls and explain a result. |
| 10 minutes | Discuss a few image pairs using the questions below. |
| 5 minutes | Each student writes a short explanation and hands it in with their two images. |

Tell students that the purpose is to explain what changed. When they are unsure, ask them to point to the part of the image they are discussing.

### Instructions for students

You will compare two PA wrist images to see how collimation affects the anatomy included.

1. Open the healthy wrist case, choose the PA view, and follow your instructor's starting setup. Check that the required anatomy is visible. Use **Save image** to save your first image.
2. Predict which anatomy will disappear first as you narrow the field. Under **Technique**, adjust one **Collimator cutoff** control until the edge of the field excludes part of that anatomy. Keep all other settings unchanged.
3. Save a second image. Record the field dimensions shown for each image so you can describe the change.
4. Compare the images. Identify what is missing in the second image and explain which adjustment would bring it back into the field.

Take turns using the controls. For the second student's comparison, return to the starting field dimensions first.

Hand in both images and a few sentences answering:

- Which anatomy was excluded?
- What did you change between the images?
- What would you adjust next, and why?

Your instructor will tell you where and when to hand in the work.

### Discuss the images

**Which image meets the coverage criteria we discussed?**

Ask students to identify the relevant anatomy and point to where it is included or excluded. A useful explanation names the anatomy and describes the field edge; it goes beyond saying that one image is "better."

**What would you change to include the missing anatomy?**

Discuss the direction in which the field needs to open. Relate the answer to the control the student changed and the coverage you teach.

**Why did we keep the other settings unchanged?**

Discuss how changing one control makes the comparison easier to explain. If a student also moved the patient or tube, ask them to return to the starting setup and repeat the comparison.

The intended comparison is about anatomy coverage. Use students' own images to discuss what happened in the simulator, including any result that differs from the class demonstration.

## Use the activity again

Once students can make and explain the comparison, you can give the same task as independent practice. Use the same instructions and your usual assignment system to collect the two images and explanation.

For another course topic, choose a case from the [Simulation Catalog](/Simulation-Catalog/overview) and try the comparison yourself first. Change the anatomy and coverage criteria in the brief to match that lesson.
