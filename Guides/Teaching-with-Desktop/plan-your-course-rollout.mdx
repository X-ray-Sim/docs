A course rollout starts with a clear place in your teaching schedule. Decide which students will use the Desktop X-ray Simulator, what they will do, and how faculty will review their work.

For your first rollout, choose one course, one cohort, and a small number of topics already on the course calendar. Name a faculty lead and schedule a review after the first two activities. Expand once you understand the preparation and support your students need.

## 1. Agree on the starting scope

Complete these decisions with the people who will teach and support the course.

| Decision | What to record |
| --- | --- |
| Course and cohort | Course name, student group, student count, and relevant prior learning. |
| Learning objectives | What students should be able to explain or demonstrate after each activity. |
| First topics | Two or three course topics and suitable simulator cases to rehearse. |
| Teaching format | Instructor demonstration, guided practice, independent work, or a sequence of these. |
| Access | Institution-owned computers, student computers, or a mix; account arrangements and technical contact. |
| Participation and feedback | Whether work is required, what students submit, who reviews it, and when feedback is returned. |
| Dates and ownership | First student session, faculty lead, preparation deadlines, and cohort review date. |

Use the [Simulation Catalog](/Simulation-Catalog/overview) to shortlist cases and check known limitations. Confirm the selected case and controls in your Desktop installation before scheduling an activity. Use your course's teaching materials to define the image evaluation criteria.

## 2. Put activities on the course calendar

Connect each simulator activity to a topic students are studying. Give students a task and an expected output, then reserve time to discuss what they found.

| Format | A practical place in the course | What faculty prepare |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Instructor demonstration | During a lecture introducing a concept. | A rehearsed case, prediction questions, and an image comparison to discuss. |
| Guided practice | In a scheduled teaching or computer lab session. | A short worksheet, working computers, and someone available to help. |
| Independent assignment | After students have practiced the workflow with an instructor. | A tested brief, an access check, a submission location, and a feedback date. |

### Example course sequence

This sequence is a starting point to adapt to your syllabus. The case and projection are chosen by the instructor.

| Course point | Student activity | Evidence for feedback |
| --- | --- | --- |
| First simulator session | Open the assigned case, follow a demonstration, and export an image. | Access check and one exported image. |
| Relevant positioning topic | Predict and compare the effect of one supported positioning adjustment. | Two images and an explanation of the observed change. |
| Relevant image evaluation topic | Compare collimation choices against the criteria taught in class. | Image pair, reasoning, and a proposed next adjustment. |

For each course activity, copy this table into your own planning document and add a row:

| Topic and date | Learning objective | Case and task | Student preparation | Submission and feedback date | Faculty owner |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| To complete | To complete | To complete | To complete | To complete | To complete |

Use the [teaching activities and templates](/Guides/Teaching-with-Desktop/activities-and-templates) to draft the student tasks.

## 3. Prepare the first cohort

The timing below is illustrative. Adjust it to your institution's installation, account, and teaching schedules.

| When | Action and suggested owner | Ready when |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 3–4 weeks before first use | Faculty lead selects topics, assigns responsibilities, and confirms the access plan with IT or the local coordinator. | The first activities have dates, owners, and a device/account plan. |
| 1–2 weeks before | Teaching faculty rehearse the activities; IT or the coordinator tests representative student computers. | An instructor has completed the same workflow students will follow, including export and submission where required. |
| Before the first teaching activity | The coordinator shares student instructions and collects a short access check. | Access issues have an owner and an agreed way for affected students to participate. |
| First scheduled session | Teaching faculty introduce the workflow and run guided practice. | Students understand the task, submission process, and where to get help. |
| After the first two activities | Faculty lead reviews student work, access issues, and preparation time. | The team records what to keep, change, and check again before expanding. |

Follow [Prepare faculty and students](/Guides/Teaching-with-Desktop/prepare-faculty-and-students) for the rehearsal checklist, access check, and student announcement template.

## 4. Copy your cohort plan

Keep the completed plan in your institution's own working documents. Replace the placeholders with your decisions.

```text
DESKTOP X-RAY SIMULATOR — COHORT PLAN

Course / cohort: [name]
Student count and prerequisites: [details]
Faculty lead: [name]
Teaching faculty: [names]
Technical / access contact: [name and contact route]

Learning objectives: [what students should explain or demonstrate]
First two activities and dates: [topic, case, format, duration, date]
Student access: [institution computers / student computers / mixed]
Account arrangements: [individual / shared station / mixed; coordinator]
Faculty rehearsal date: [date]
Student access check deadline: [date]
Plan for unresolved access issues: [arrangement and owner]

Expected student work: [images, explanation, or other evidence]
Submission location and deadline: [institution's usual assignment process]
Feedback owner and date: [name and date]
What a successful first rollout would look like: [observable criteria]
Cohort review date and participants: [date and names]
Decision after review: [continue / revise / expand; actions and owners]
```

Record account arrangements here; share passwords only through your institution's approved channel.

## 5. Review before expanding

Agree on simple measures before the first session. Use the access check, submitted work, and a short faculty discussion to review the rollout.

| Review question | Evidence to collect |
| --- | --- |
| Could students participate? | Number who completed the access check, unresolved issues, and how those issues were handled. |
| Did the activity fit the course? | Whether students had the prerequisites and whether the task fit the scheduled time. |
| What did students understand? | A sample of image comparisons and explanations reviewed against the stated learning objective. |
| Was delivery manageable? | Faculty preparation time, support questions, and time needed to give feedback. |
| What should change next? | One or two specific changes, each with an owner and a date. |

A completed task shows participation; the student's explanation gives faculty evidence to discuss learning. Record both when reviewing the first cohort.

Before adding another cohort, update the instructions and activities with what you learned. Keep institution-specific dates and decisions in your cohort plan so the reusable teaching materials stay easy to maintain.
