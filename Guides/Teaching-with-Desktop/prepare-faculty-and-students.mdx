Before class, try your [chosen activity](/Guides/Teaching-with-Desktop/activities-and-templates) and save any images you want to discuss.

For a demonstration or for making slides, you only need to prepare your own computer and login. If students will use the simulator themselves, arrange their access too.

## 1. Try the activity yourself

Choose a case and projection that match your topic. Work through the task using the image criteria you teach.

Check that you can:

- Find the case and the controls you will use.
- Make the intended change and explain its effect on the image.
- Save an image and find the file on the computer.

Use the [Desktop quick start](/Guides/Introduction/getting-started-Desktop-x-ray) if you need help. Save your example images so you can still discuss the task if a live demonstration is interrupted.

Decide how long to allow after trying the task. Students will also need time to ask questions and become familiar with the controls.

## 2. Arrange computers and logins

**For a class demonstration:** test your computer, login, and projector in the teaching room. Check that students will be able to see the image features you plan to discuss.

**For student practice on institution computers:** tell your IT team which rooms or computers you need and when. Share the [IT setup information](/Procurement/Security%20and%20IT%20approval) and ask them to follow the Desktop guidance. Try the simulator on one of the computers students will use.

**For students' own computers:** share the [Desktop requirements](/Tech/minimum-requirements-for-running-the-desktop-xray-simulator) and [installation guide](/Tech/Setup-and-installation-guides/Desktop-X-ray-Simulator-Setup) in advance. Arrange access to an institution computer for anyone who needs it.

You need an X-ray Simulator login to open the application. Ask the person coordinating it at your institution to arrange the logins. If that is you, [Accounts and Login](/Guides/Introduction/accounts-and-login) explains how to get accounts from VitaSim. Internet access is needed when logging in.

Before student practice begins, ask students to open the chosen case and save an image. Give them a deadline to report any difficulty so there is time to help.

## 3. Tell students what to expect

Share the topic, what to review beforehand, how students will work, and what they will hand in. For homework, include the submission location and deadline.

You can adapt this message for a first activity in pairs. Fill in the bracketed details and attach the computer and login instructions agreed with your institution.

> **Before our first X-ray Simulator class**
>
> In our class on **[date and location]**, we will practise **[region and projection]** using **[case]**.
>
> Before **[access-check deadline]**, follow the attached computer and login instructions. Open the Desktop X-ray Simulator, find the case, and try saving an image.
>
> If you get stuck, contact **[help contact]** and tell us which step you reached.
>
> Review **[course material and image criteria]** before class. You will work in pairs and take turns making an image and explaining your choices.
>
> Each of you will hand in an image from your turn and a short explanation of one decision at **[submission place and deadline]**. We will discuss examples together.

For an assignment students will complete independently, use the [image-making assignment brief](/Guides/Teaching-with-Desktop/activities-and-templates#set-an-image-making-assignment).

## Know where to get help

Ask your institution's IT team about installation or computer access, and your simulator coordinator about logins.

For a problem with the application, contact [VitaSim support](/Support/support). Include the case name, what you were trying to do, and any error message.
