Before the first class, try the activity yourself and make sure students can open the simulator. You can then spend the session helping them explore and explain their images.

These steps use the [wrist collimation activity](/Guides/Teaching-with-Desktop/activities-and-templates#wrist-collimation-what-is-missing) as an example.

## 1. Try the activity yourself

Open the healthy wrist case and follow the student instructions. Save the two images so you can use them in your demonstration and discussion.

Check that you can:

- Find the case and controls students will use.
- Make the comparison and explain what changes in the images.
- Save an image and find the file on the computer.

Use the [Desktop quick start](/Guides/Introduction/getting-started-Desktop-x-ray) if you need help with the controls or saving images.

Decide how long to allow after trying the task. Students will also need time to ask questions and become familiar with the simulator.

## 2. Arrange computers and logins

**For institution computers:** tell your IT team which rooms or computers the class will use and when you need them ready. Share the [IT setup information](/Procurement/Security%20and%20IT%20approval) and ask them to follow the Desktop guidance. Try the simulator on one of the computers students will use before the class.

**For students' own computers:** share the [Desktop requirements](/Tech/minimum-requirements-for-running-the-desktop-xray-simulator) and [installation guide](/Tech/Setup-and-installation-guides/Desktop-X-ray-Simulator-Setup) in advance. Arrange access to an institution computer for anyone who needs it.

Students also need an X-ray Simulator account. Ask the person coordinating the simulator at your institution to arrange the logins. If you are that person, [Accounts and Login](/Guides/Introduction/accounts-and-login) explains how to get accounts from VitaSim. Internet access is needed when logging in.

Before the first activity, ask students to open the simulator and the healthy wrist case. If they will hand in images, ask them to save one and check that they can find it. Give them a deadline to report any difficulty so there is time to help.

## 3. Tell students what to expect

Share when and where the activity takes place, what to review beforehand, and what students will hand in.

You can adapt this message for the wrist activity. Fill in the four bracketed details and include the computer and login instructions agreed with your institution.

> **Before our first X-ray Simulator class**
>
> In our class on **[date and location]**, we will explore how collimation changes the anatomy included in a wrist image.
>
> Before **[access-check deadline]**, follow the computer and login instructions attached to this message. Open the Desktop X-ray Simulator, find the healthy wrist case, and try saving an image.
>
> If you get stuck, contact **[help contact]** and tell us which step you reached.
>
> Review the PA wrist anatomy and image coverage discussed in class. During the activity, you will work in pairs, compare two images, and explain what you would change.
>
> Hand in your images and a short explanation at **[submission place and deadline]**. We will discuss selected examples together.

## Know where to get help

Ask your institution's IT team about installation or computer access, and your simulator coordinator about the logins students should use.

For a problem with the application, contact [VitaSim support](/Support/support). Include the case name, what you were trying to do, and any error message.
